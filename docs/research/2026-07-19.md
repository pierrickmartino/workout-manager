# Research Report — 2026-07-19

Focused research sweep for **workout-manager** on three fronts requested for
this cycle: (1) **gaps versus other market contestants**, (2) **UX patterns for
visual workout builders** (our F4 Protocol Builder), and (3) **UX best practices
for the PWA** (`apps/web`). This report reads the market and the platform against
our load-bearing invariants and the current feature state
(`docs/pulse-feature-gap.md`: every Pulse screen is now feature-backed; remaining
work is capability wiring, not fresh domain). No source code, ADRs, or
`CONTEXT.md` were touched in producing this report.

> CVE-2026-48710, Next.js May-2026). Those remain open Next Actions and are **not**

## Executive Summary

- **The market has moved the goalposts to *recovery-aware, wearable-driven,
  conversational* coaching — and we deliberately don't play there.** The 2026
  consensus critique is that most apps are "loggers, not coaches": they don't
  change the session in front of you based on what your body told a wearable this
  morning. Leaders (SensAI, Athlytic, Cora, WHOOP-tier) now read HRV/sleep/RHR
  **daily** and rewrite today's session, then *explain why* via an LLM. Our stance
  is the opposite by design: **self-paced, calendar-free, no "today," Readiness is
  a 3-state signal not a score** (ADR-0001/0008). This is a genuine strategic
  fork, not a bug — but it should be a **conscious** position, so it's logged as
  the headline Question, not a Finding-to-fix.
- **Our actual differentiators line up with named competitor gaps.** Fitbod has
  *zero cardio/conditioning integration* and no conversational layer; Freeletics'
  personalization "feels generic after a few weeks" with no real progression
  structure; Caliber is strength-only and expensive. Our **type-neutral**
  spine — multi-`TRAINING_TYPES` (strength/cardio/hiit/yoga/mobility), the
  type-neutral XP/Achievement engine (ADR-0018), the honest typed `Load`
  (ADR-0010) — is a defensible wedge against exactly these weaknesses. The gap is
  **not capability, it's narrative**: nothing in the product currently *says*
  "trains every modality honestly."
- **Visual workout-builder UX best practice = direct-manipulation drag-and-drop
  with explicit drop zones, keyboard access, and clear affordances.** Our F4
  Builder already has the hard part (staged draft, tail-only mutation, atomic
  DEPLOY, pick-only Library). The open UX gap is the *manipulation layer*: the
  reorder today is a `position` field, and ADR-0023 has already committed to
  **drag-to-reorder + drag-to-group supersets**. The research reinforces the
  accessibility and drop-zone-feedback requirements that decision now needs to
  satisfy.
- **PWA reality check: iOS is where our client-side Live Session is most
  exposed.** iOS caps *script-writable storage* (localStorage/IndexedDB) at a
  **7-day eviction window** for unused PWAs and offers **no Background Sync**. Our
  **ephemeral, localStorage-only Live Session** (ADR-0012, single slot, nothing
  hits the backend until finish) is architecturally aligned with "no background
  sync" — but the 7-day/eviction cap is a **real data-loss risk** for a paused
  in-progress session and for the `ResumeSessionBanner`. This is the one place the
  PWA platform actively threatens a shipped invariant.
- **No open issues on the tracker** (`list_issues` → 0). The market/UX/PWA inputs
  here are forward-looking fuel for the next issues to be written, chiefly the
  gamification/PR **Home & Analytics fan-out** and the ADR-0023 Builder
  manipulation layer.

## Findings for Active Issues

The issue tracker currently has **zero open issues**. There is therefore no
`ready-for-agent` work to pick up. The "active" work in flight is the
already-decided-but-unbuilt items recorded in `docs/pulse-feature-gap.md` and
yesterday's ADR-0023; the findings below map this cycle's research onto those.

### F-A · Builder manipulation layer (ADR-0023 already decided; UX now specified)

**State:** decided (2026-07-18 grill → ADR-0023: supersets as a round-major
grouping overlay, AI generator emits them, drag-to-reorder lands alongside
drag-to-group, suppressed for Sensitive-Constraint users via a shared validator).
Not yet built.

This cycle's builder-UX research turns that decision into concrete acceptance
criteria for whoever writes the issue:

- **Direct manipulation is the expected affordance** for "build and rearrange
  visually instead of editing a form." Coaches' tools (Movement, CoachingPortal,
  Hevy Coach) all use drag-to-move exercises between days and drag-to-reorder
  within a day as the default gesture. Our current `position`-field reorder is the
  *data model*; the gesture is the missing UX.
- **Drop zones must give escalating visual feedback** — an inert list that only
  commits on release is the documented anti-pattern. Show the insertion point,
  intensify the target as the dragged item nears, and use microcopy to foreshadow
  the drop (e.g. "release to group into a superset").
- **Keyboard + touch parity is non-negotiable**, not a polish item. Drag-and-drop
  that is mouse-only fails accessibility and fails on the phone — which for a PWA
  gym app *is* the primary device. Every drag operation needs a keyboard path
  (move up/down, group/ungroup) and a large touch target.
- **Constraint:** all of this stays inside ADR-0020's **un-performed tail** — a
  performed Session is settled record and must never be draggable/reorderable. The
  drag layer must read the frozen prefix and refuse drops into it, mirroring the
  server-enforced `deploy_validation.py` gate on the client.

### F-B · Gamification / PR fan-out to Home & Analytics (named, unbuilt)

**State:** the XP/Level/Streak/Achievement engine (ADR-0018/0019) and the
PR/1RM/volume engine (ADR-0010) are shipped and surfaced on Profile/Analytics/
Exercise Detail; the **Home + Analytics fan-out** is the named remaining slice.

Market relevance: the 2026 retention playbook still leans on visible
progress/momentum, and our Home is the highest-traffic surface. Surfacing
Level/XP/Streak and the top PR on Home — **off the same read-time endpoints, no
new domain** — is the cheapest market-competitive win available and directly
answers the "feels generic after a few weeks" critique levelled at Freeletics.
This is capability wiring, exactly the low-risk category the gap analysis
identifies as all that remains.

## Security Risks

Scoped to this report's three topics (market/UX/PWA). The three dependency-bump
advisories from **2026-07-18** (Clerk 9.1, Starlette, Next.js May-2026) are still
open and still the highest-severity items overall — they are carried forward as
Next Actions, not repeated here.

### 1. HIGH — Client-side Live Session storage is a confidentiality + integrity surface
- **Our exposure:** the Live Session persists to a single `localStorage` slot
  (ADR-0012) and the gap analysis notes timers/state survive refresh/crash/lock.
  `localStorage` is **plain-text, same-origin, readable by any script on the
  page** and has **no expiry we control**. Any XSS on `apps/web` can read a paused
  session's contents or write a forged one that the `ResumeSessionBanner`
  faithfully hydrates.
- **Why it matters here:** the PWA is the whole client; a single injected script
  (third-party dep, a future embed) reads/writes the slot. Treat the localStorage
  Live Session as **untrusted input on hydration** — `fetchLiveSession` /
  resume-path should validate the stored shape (it crosses a trust boundary the
  same way an API response does), never `JSON.parse`-and-trust.
- **Action (research-only report — logged, not applied):** confirm the resume path
  schema-validates the stored slot; keep no secret/PII in it (it holds workout
  performance, which is low-sensitivity but still user data); ensure our CSP is
  strict enough that an XSS can't trivially exfiltrate it.

### 2. MEDIUM — Service-worker cache poisoning / stale-auth via offline caching
- **Our exposure:** a PWA's value proposition is a service worker + cache, but a
  mis-scoped cache is a classic footgun — caching an **authenticated** response
  and later serving it to a different session, or caching an app-shell that pins a
  stale/vulnerable bundle past a security release. This intersects directly with
  yesterday's Next.js cache-poisoning advisory.
- **Why it matters here:** ADR-0022's transport seam is deliberately
  `cache: "no-store"` for per-user authed reads. Any service-worker caching we add
  for offline must **exclude the authed `/api/*` reads** and cache only static
  shell/assets, or we reintroduce cross-user leakage the seam was designed to
  prevent. iOS's 7-day cache purge is a *reliability* problem (below); serving a
  **wrong-user** cached read would be a *security* one.
- **Action:** if/when a service worker is added, adopt a network-first (or
  no-cache) policy for `/api/*` and cache-first only for versioned static assets;
  never cache responses carrying the Clerk JWT context.

### 3. MEDIUM — Web Push / install-prompt permission hygiene
- **Our exposure:** if we pursue Web Push (installed Android PWAs see 7–15% open
  rates vs 1–4% email — a real retention lever), the documented failure mode is
  **requesting notification/push permission on load**, which gets denied and
  permanently poisons the prompt. Push also requires iOS 16.4+ and only for an
  *installed* PWA, outside the EU.
- **Action:** gate any push/install prompt behind an earned-engagement moment
  (e.g. after a completed Logged Session), never on first paint; feature-detect and
  fall back to in-app cues where unsupported. **Note the domain guardrail:** any
  notification copy must not become a **daily-streak** nag — ADR-0019 chose a
  *weekly* Streak precisely to avoid pressuring users to train through legitimate
  rest days. A "don't break your streak today" push would silently violate that
  decision.

## Implementation References

Curated inputs for upcoming issues. Nothing here is a directive to change code
now.

### Market positioning (competitor gaps → our wedge)
- **Fitbod (4.81★, 272k ratings; $15.99/mo as of Jun 2026):** strong equipment-
  aware strength generation, but **no cardio/conditioning integration**, **can't
  see sleep/HRV/stress**, and **no conversational coaching** — a "meaningful
  ceiling" per 2026 reviews. Our multi-type spine covers the cardio gap by design.
- **Freeletics (4.64★; ~$34.99 bundle):** location-flexible, high variety, low
  setup friction, but **surface-level personalization** that "feels generic after
  a few weeks," **no real progression structure**, no fatigue/interference
  modelling. Our Protocol progression (ADR-0004/0013) + honest PR engine is the
  counter.
- **Caliber (£40+/mo):** AI + optional human coach, but **strength-only** and
  **expensive**, feedback limited to scheduled check-ins.
- **The industry critique to internalize:** "most workout apps in 2026 are
  loggers, not coaches" — they don't adapt the session to same-day recovery
  state. Adaptive-training writers frame this as **four levels of fatigue-aware
  training, and most apps stop at level 2**. Our **Readiness** 3-state signal
  (ADR-0008) is a deliberate *level-2-ish, honest* stance: we don't own a recovery
  clock, so we don't fake one. Worth stating this explicitly in positioning rather
  than looking like an omission.

### Wearable / HRV trend (the biggest strategic fork)
- Consumer wearable HRV is now **clinical-grade** (Oura Gen 4 ~99% ECG
  concordance; WHOOP 4.0 ~94% per the 2025 Dial et al. validation), so "the data
  isn't trustworthy" is no longer a defensible reason to abstain. The leaders'
  differentiation is **LLM reasoning across HRV+sleep+load** and *explaining* the
  adjustment conversationally.
- **Tension with our invariants:** a daily wearable-driven "rewrite today's
  session" model is fundamentally a **calendar/"today"** model — it collides with
  ADR-0001 (self-paced, calendar-free, no "today"), the **read-time-projection**
  rule (ADR-0018/0019), and the **Load-is-typed** rule. It is *not* a drop-in
  feature; it would be a second product posture. This is the central Question,
  below — do we hold the honest self-paced line as our differentiator, or open a
  bounded wearable lane that respects the invariants (e.g. HRV informing the
  existing **3-state Readiness** signal *without* introducing a recovery
  percentage or a dated schedule)?

### Visual workout-builder UX (for the ADR-0023 manipulation layer)
- **Drag-and-drop is for organize/reorder/build/customize** interactions — exactly
  the Builder's job. Best-practice checklist distilled from this cycle's sources:
  clear drag handles, **explicit drop zones with escalating feedback**, a live
  insertion indicator, microcopy foreshadowing the drop, an undo/cancel path,
  **keyboard operability**, and **mobile touch** as a first-class (not fallback)
  input.
- **Superset grouping (ADR-0023)** maps to the well-worn "group items into a
  container" DnD pattern (Kanban-card-into-column / builder-block-into-group). The
  round-major overlay should read as a visible container the user drags members
  into — consistent with our decision that it's *not* a mere A/B/C badge.
- **Keep it invariant-safe:** tail-only editability (ADR-0020), typed `Load`
  entry reusing the log form's kind-picker (already done in F4), and **superset
  suppression for Sensitive-Constraint users** enforced in the shared validator
  (ADR-0023 / safety-cache-bypass posture, ADR-0003).

### PWA UX best practices (for `apps/web`)
- **Performance is the foundation everything else compounds on.** Target Core Web
  Vitals — LCP, **INP**, CLS — and **test on a throttled low-end Android** (Moto
  G4-class), not a dev laptop; a 1s mobile delay can cut conversion ~20%. This is
  the highest-ROI PWA work and it's measurement, not redesign.
- **Offline-first is a UX philosophy, not a feature:** show cached data
  immediately, sync in background where supported, and **communicate sync status**
  — offline states should be meaningful UI, never an error page. For us the honest
  scope is small: the Live Session is already offline-capable client-side; a read
  cache for the app shell + last-seen Home/Protocol is the natural first offline
  slice.
- **Installability criteria are all-or-nothing:** HTTPS + a registered service
  worker + a valid manifest (icons, `start_url`) — miss one and Chrome *silently*
  suppresses "Add to Home Screen." Audit the manifest/SW before assuming install
  works.
- **iOS is the constraint that shapes the roadmap** (see PWA-specific risks
  below). Design for graceful cross-platform degradation: **feature-detect** push,
  haptics, background sync, and fall back to in-app cues.

### PWA on iOS — platform limits that touch our shipped features
- **No Background Sync API on iOS** (all iOS browsers are WebKit, including
  "Chrome"). Our Live Session posts *nothing* until finish (ADR-0012), so we're
  aligned — but any future "queue this log and send when back online" must be
  handled on **next foreground**, not via background sync.
- **~7-day script-writable storage eviction** and tight quotas (Cache API ~50MB;
  IndexedDB/localStorage auto-cleared when a PWA is unused for weeks). **Direct
  risk:** a paused in-progress Live Session held only in `localStorage` can be
  **evicted**, and the `ResumeSessionBanner` would then have nothing to resume.
  Mitigations to weigh (design, not decided): treat the local slot as best-effort,
  make resume degrade gracefully to "start fresh," and consider a *finished*-only
  server persistence boundary (already the case) so nothing durable depends on the
  volatile slot.
- **Push:** iOS 16.4+, installed-PWA-only, gated behind the install step — so push
  is a smaller reachable audience on iOS than Android; plan copy/UX for the
  installed-only case.

## Questions

1. **Wearable/recovery posture — the headline strategic call.** The market's
   differentiation is now daily HRV/sleep-driven session rewriting + conversational
   "why." That model is calendar-bound and collides with ADR-0001/0008/0018. Do we
   (a) **hold the honest self-paced line** and market *that* as the differentiator
   against the "loggers, not coaches / faked recovery clocks" field, or (b) open a
   **bounded, invariant-respecting lane** where an optional wearable signal *only*
   informs the existing 3-state **Readiness** (no recovery %, no dated schedule, no
   "today")? This likely deserves a grill session + ADR before any issue.
2. **Type-neutral positioning — narrate the wedge?** Our real edge over
   Fitbod/Freeletics/Caliber is honest multi-modality (cardio/hiit/yoga/mobility
   as first-class, type-neutral XP/Achievements). Should the next product cycle
   include a **positioning/onboarding** slice that actually *says* this (the FO1
   welcome/tagline is still ❌ in the gap analysis), rather than leaving it implicit?
3. **Builder manipulation scope (ADR-0023).** Confirm the first cut ships
   **both** drag-to-reorder and drag-to-group supersets together (as ADR-0023
   reads), with full keyboard + touch parity as acceptance criteria — or does
   drag-to-reorder land first and grouping follow? The accessibility bar is the
   same either way.
4. **Do we add a service worker at all this cycle?** Offline/install/push are the
   PWA levers, but each carries the cache-poisoning and permission-hygiene risks
   above. Is a **minimal shell-only service worker + manifest audit** (no `/api/*`
   caching) in scope, or do we stay a no-SW installable-later PWA for now?
5. **iOS local-storage durability for the Live Session.** Are we comfortable that
   a paused Live Session is **best-effort** on iOS (7-day eviction), or do we want
   a design slice that hardens resume (graceful "start fresh" + clearer messaging)
   before it bites a real user mid-cut?
